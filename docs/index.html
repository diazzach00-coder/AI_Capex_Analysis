The AI money loop

Who is financing the AI buildout, where the money ends up, and what has to happen for it to come back. Every number traces to a row in data/; hover any mark for its value.

1. Where the announced money goes

Every announced commitment in data/deals.csv that has a public dollar figure, drawn from the party that pays to the party that receives, then sorted by what happens to it. A dollar is a round trip when its edge sits on a closed loop in the deal graph (a hyperscaler invests in a lab that commits to buy its cloud). It is carried as debt when it is a neocloud bond or convertible. Everything else is one-way: real money, but nothing in the public record yet says whether it earns a return.

2. Is the capex earning anything yet?

The only place a return can show up is the revenue line of the business the capex was built for. For each hyperscaler: quarterly capital expenditure (bars) against the revenue of its cloud or ad segment (line), both in billions of dollars on one axis. The tile above each panel compares trailing-four-quarter capex with the year-over-year change in trailing-four-quarter segment revenue: dollars of new segment revenue per dollar of capex.

Segment revenueCapex (company-wide)

Capex is company-wide and includes non-AI spend; segment revenue is one segment. Microsoft does not disclose Azure revenue in dollars, so its panel uses the Intelligent Cloud segment. A ratio near or above 1.0 means the segment is adding revenue at the pace of the spend; below it, the spend is running ahead of any visible return. Segment figures are hand-entered from each company's 8-K earnings exhibit; see data/segment_revenue.csv.

5. What has to happen

A stylized break-even. The four hyperscalers guide to a combined annual capex run-rate; that hardware wears out over a useful life and the money behind it needs a return. Dividing by the operating margin earned on the new revenue gives the annual revenue the AI business must add just to stand still.
